# Short Squeeze Project Video Presentation Design

## Purpose

Create a concise presentation and matching narration that explain the completed Short Squeeze Research Screener to a course evaluator or professional reviewer. The presentation should demonstrate the finished product, the research methodology behind it, the evidence supporting its quality, and the limits of its claims.

## Deliverables

- A seven-slide widescreen PowerPoint presentation.
- A polished Word narration script lasting approximately six to seven minutes.
- Matching speaker notes embedded in the PowerPoint.
- Application screenshots captured from the local deterministic frozen demonstration.
- Recording cues that identify when to advance slides or briefly show the live interface.

## Narrative Approach

Use a balanced project story rather than a purely technical architecture review or a personal reflection. The presentation will connect three layers:

1. The original internship objective: create a tool for identifying potential short-squeeze candidates.
2. The finished system: a read-only research screener that compares transparent methodologies using timestamped evidence.
3. The professional lesson: evidence quality, reproducibility, and honest uncertainty are more valuable than overstating prediction.

The project must never be described as a validated trading system. Claims will follow the current repository documentation: no order execution, no account access, no trading recommendations, and no completed predictive validation, backtesting, profit-and-loss analysis, or threshold optimization.

## Slide Structure

### Slide 1 - Title and Purpose

Introduce the Short Squeeze Research Screener and frame it as an evidence-first research project. Establish the central question: how can potential candidates be evaluated without hiding missing or conflicting evidence?

### Slide 2 - The Problem and Original Goal

Explain what a short squeeze is at a high level, why candidate screening is difficult, and how the internship goal evolved from prediction language toward a more defensible research tool. Mention the early data-collection mistake described in the original reflection and the resulting focus on smaller-cap candidates.

### Slide 3 - What the Finished Application Does

Show the product workflow: collect provider evidence, normalize it, preserve provenance and missingness, compare independent methodologies, and present results through a read-only dashboard and stable API. Include the three runtime modes and the deterministic 13-candidate frozen demonstration.

### Slide 4 - Evidence-Gated Methodology

Present the two scoring dimensions: pressure and ignition. Summarize the principal inputs, the 65 percent supported-weight floor, and the classifications PRIME, SUBPRIME, WATCH, NOT QUALIFIED, UNEVALUABLE, and CONFLICTED. Emphasize that unknown evidence is not converted to zero or failure.

### Slide 5 - Interface Demonstration

Use actual application screenshots. Guide the viewer through the candidate list, evidence status, methodology comparison, provider health, and any visible explanation or provenance details. The script will include concise screen-navigation cues suitable for either a prerecorded screen capture or a slide-only presentation.

### Slide 6 - Verification and Case Evidence

Show the scale of engineering verification and the BIYA historical case study. Use current repository evidence where available and clearly date any historical test count. State that BIYA later moved more than 127 percent from a defensible detection boundary in retained data, while explaining that this confirms a later price outcome, not the original methodology or a profitable trade.

### Slide 7 - Limitations, Learning, and Next Steps

Close with the project's explicit limitations, the professional lessons about data quality and communication, and the next logical work: acquire broader point-in-time historical evidence, preregister evaluation policies, and conduct leakage-controlled validation before making predictive claims.

## Visual Direction

- Widescreen 16:9 layout.
- Dominant charcoal background with warm off-white content surfaces.
- Amber accent for research pressure and teal accent for ignition/evidence quality.
- Repeated motif of evidence cards, status chips, and subtle market-grid geometry.
- Large typography, short slide copy, and strong visual hierarchy.
- Actual product screenshots rather than stock photography.
- No decorative title underlines, trading hype, green/red buy-sell language, or unsupported performance graphics.

## Narration Design

Target approximately 850 to 1,000 spoken words, with natural pacing and a total duration of six to seven minutes. Each section will include:

- Slide number and title.
- Approximate duration.
- Exact narration in a conversational first-person voice.
- Optional on-screen or live-demo cue.
- A brief transition to the next slide.

The narration must expand on the slide rather than read it verbatim. The tone should be confident, honest, and understandable to a non-specialist evaluator.

## Source Basis

The content will be based on the maintained repository documentation, the original final reflection PDF, the methodology definition, the current frozen-demo behavior, release verification reports, and the BIYA historical outcome report. No unsupported outside statistics are needed.

## Quality and Acceptance Criteria

- Every slide has a meaningful visual element.
- Slide content and narration remain synchronized.
- The local application screenshots are legible at presentation scale.
- No text is clipped, overlapped, or smaller than a comfortable presentation size.
- The PowerPoint contains the narration as speaker notes.
- The Word script renders cleanly and is easy to read while recording.
- Technical claims can be traced to the project files and preserve the documented interpretation boundaries.
- Both files pass text extraction and visual render review, followed by at least one correction-and-reverification cycle.

## Scope Boundaries

This work does not modify the application, deploy the project, create a recorded video, or claim trading performance. It provides a complete, ready-to-record presentation package.
