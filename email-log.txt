Actually I didn't get it from TD Ameritrade.

It's an indicator you calculate yourself from regular daily price data (high/low/close). I'm already pulling that data from Interactive Brokers for other stuff like RSI, so I just run the same price history through the TTM Squeeze formula too. If Schwab ends up being the active source instead of IB it works the same way off Schwab's data.

The formula itself: you compute Bollinger Bands (a band around price based on recent volatility) and Keltner Channels (a similar band but based on average trading range). When the Bollinger Bands squeeze in tight enough to sit entirely inside the Keltner Channels, that means volatility has really dried up and the stock is "coiled," which is usually right before a big move, and that's the squeeze signal. There's also a momentum piece that tells you which direction it's leaning.

It's originally John Carter's indicator, popularized on TD Ameritrade's old thinkorswim platform, which is probably why it's associated with them. But what I'm using is the standard open-source version of the formula, not their exact proprietary one. The squeeze on/off part is solid, I just treat the momentum number as directional rather than something precise.

Let me know if you need anything else.
